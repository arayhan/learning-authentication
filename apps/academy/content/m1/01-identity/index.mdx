<LessonSteps>
  <Step id="hook" title="Hook">
    You type a password, the page changes, and the app greets you by name. Two separate questions were just answered, and mixing them up is behind a large share of real-world breaches.

    <Analogy title="The hotel">
      At check-in the desk checks your passport and decides you really are Sam: that is authentication. Your key card then opens only the floors Sam booked: that is authorization.
    </Analogy>
  </Step>

  <Step id="concept" title="Concept">
    Three words get blurred together, so pin them down.

    - **Identity** is the account the system believes you are acting as.
    - **Authentication** (authn) is the proof that the caller owns that identity: a password, a passkey, a code.
    - **Authorization** (authz) is the decision about what that identity may do, made again on every request.

    Here is the whole round trip. Step through it, and click an arrow with a dotted label to read the raw message.

    <SequenceFlow flow={flows.whoIsWho} />

    Authentication happens once per login. Authorization happens on every request, because roles change and every resource has a different owner.

    | | Authentication | Authorization |
    |---|---|---|
    | Question | Who are you? | May you do this? |
    | Input | Password, passkey, code | Identity plus a rule or policy |
    | Frequency | At login | Every request |
    | Failure | `401 Unauthorized` | `403 Forbidden` |

    <Callout type="security">
      A 401 means "I do not know who you are". A 403 means "I know who you are, and the answer is no". Returning the wrong one hides bugs and leaks information.
    </Callout>

    <Callout type="info">
      Worth asking in your own SSO setup: which component answers "who is this?" (your **sso-backend**?), and where does each platform (or `core-api`) decide what that person may do? A valid SSO login should never mean "allowed everywhere".
    </Callout>
  </Step>
